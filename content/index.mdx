# Lancetta

**Lancetta** is a native macOS menu-bar app that tells you **when and how to use your coding agents**. A *lancetta* is the hand of an instrument — the needle that says where you are, and which way to go.

![The Lancetta panel: three suggestions first, the most urgent open: at this pace Claude stops in 51m, then waits 1h18m for its reset, with what helps (lower the effort, a lighter model for routine work, no free reset to fall back on, move the next tasks to Codex) and what it rests on; then Codex has room, and Maintenance found 4 warnings. Under them the window in brief: Claude’s card open on its 5-hour, 7-day and Fable bars, Codex at 20%, Usage and Maintenance](/screenshot-menu-dark.png)

A percentage tells you what is gone, and leaves the decision to you. Lancetta makes it with you. From every reading of **Codex** and **Claude Code**, and a scan of what the agents load at every start, it says what to do next:

- **push on**: a window about to reset with most of it unused is quota you lose, so spend it;
- **slow down**: at this pace an agent stops before its window resets, so lower the effort, give routine work to a lighter model, or move to the other agent while it has room;
- **fix the setup**: what every session loads before your first prompt, and what is wrong in it;
- **reclaim**: the background trees the agents leave behind, which nothing else will ever close.

Each suggestion says what it rests on: the percentage each account reports, how fast it has been moving, when the window resets, the effort set in each agent. Those numbers are all on screen too, with the bucket that refused named rather than averaged away, every reading carrying its own age, and not one token spent to find out. See [Suggestions](/docs/suggestions).

It says all this on three surfaces, so the one you need is the one already in front of you:

- the **panel** under the menu-bar item, which opens on the most urgent suggestions;
- the **island under the notch**, whose bar turns red when an agent has stopped or will within the hour, and which says what to do without the mouse;
- the **window**, with every suggestion and what it rests on: usage over weeks, each agent in detail, the processes, and [Maintenance](/docs/maintenance).

## Why it exists

Two failures on one afternoon, both invisible without a terminal and a reason to suspect.

**The quota readout was wrong and it looked right.** A status line showed `0%` while the tool itself reported 59% remaining and two and three quarter hours to reset. Three separate defects had stacked up, and each one rendered as a plausible number: a reading three hours old with nothing saying so, a refused turn that records no limits at all so the search fell back to an older comfortable figure, and a reset time already in the past drawn as zero. Fixing it in a shell script fixed it for one terminal. The numbers belong somewhere that is on screen whatever window is in front.

**Twenty-eight orphaned processes were holding 2.68 GB.** Fourteen background trees, every one of them adopted by the system, twelve serving working directories that had already been deleted, the oldest alive for a day and eight hours. Reaped by hand, that came back to four processes and 436 MB. That is now a suggestion, a card in the panel and a pane in the window — see [The processes they leave behind](/docs/memory).

## Reading a quota costs nothing

This is the constraint the whole app is built around, and it was measured rather than assumed.

A monitor that spends quota in order to display quota is self-defeating, and the failure would be invisible: a few hundred tokens per poll, every forty-five seconds, is a real bite out of a five-hour window, and nothing in the UI would say so.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime tokens | 318,009,023 | 318,009,023 |
| Percentages used | 33% / 13% | 33% / 13% |

Twelve account reads on one connection moved nothing. And the instrument works, which is the half that makes the zero mean something — the same counter moved by 29,188,602 across a day of ordinary use. A counter that *cannot* move is indistinguishable from one that did not.

Claude Code's side is the same kind of read: Lancetta asks your Claude account how much is left, with the sign-in Claude Code already keeps on your Mac — the question Claude Code's own `/usage` asks. No model is in the loop, so nothing is billed.

**Asking a model how much quota is left is a turn, and it would cost tokens on every poll.** It is the easiest thing to build and the one route this app will never take. See [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads).

## An unknown is never a number

The defect that started this project was a display rendering an unknown as `0%`, and a three-hour-old reading as current. So:

- a window with no reading is drawn as *unknown*, never as zero;
- every reading carries the time it was taken;
- a source that has fallen behind its own cadence says so, because a frozen number is itself an event.

## Requirements

- macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later
- At least one of **Codex** or **Claude Code** installed. Lancetta finds them itself; nothing has to be configured by hand.
- The notch panel needs a Mac that has a notch. On every other Mac the island never appears and the menu-bar item behaves identically.
