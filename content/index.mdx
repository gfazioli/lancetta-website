# Lancetta

**Lancetta** is a native macOS menu-bar app that tells you where your coding agents stand. A *lancetta* is the hand of an instrument — the needle that says where you are.

![The Lancetta menu: Codex and Claude Code, each with a 5-hour and a 7-day quota window and the time it resets](/screenshot-menu-dark.png)

It does one job: **how much of each agent's quota is left, and when it comes back.** The 5-hour and the 7-day window for **Codex** and **Claude Code**, with the bucket that refused named rather than averaged away, every reading carrying its own age, and not one token spent to find out.

It puts that reading on three surfaces, so the one you need is the one already in front of you:

- the **menu bar**, for the glance;
- the **island under the notch**, for the glance that does not need the mouse;
- the **window**, for usage over weeks and each agent in detail.

Agents also leave background processes running long after they are done. Lancetta lists those too and closes the orphans on your say-so — a footnote to the job, not a second one. See [The processes they leave behind](/docs/memory).

## Why it exists

Two failures on one afternoon, both invisible without a terminal and a reason to suspect.

**The quota readout was wrong and it looked right.** A status line showed `0%` while the tool itself reported 59% remaining and two and three quarter hours to reset. Three separate defects had stacked up, and each one rendered as a plausible number: a reading three hours old with nothing saying so, a refused turn that records no limits at all so the search fell back to an older comfortable figure, and a reset time already in the past drawn as zero. Fixing it in a shell script fixed it for one terminal. The numbers belong somewhere that is on screen whatever window is in front.

**Twenty-eight orphaned processes were holding 2.68 GB.** Fourteen background trees, every one of them adopted by the system, twelve serving working directories that had already been deleted, the oldest alive for a day and eight hours. Reaped by hand, that came back to four processes and 436 MB. That is now a line in the menu and a pane in the window — see [The processes they leave behind](/docs/memory).

## Reading a quota costs nothing

This is the constraint the whole app is built around, and it was measured rather than assumed.

A monitor that spends quota in order to display quota is self-defeating, and the failure would be invisible: a few hundred tokens per poll, every forty-five seconds, is a real bite out of a five-hour window, and nothing in the UI would say so.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime tokens | 318,009,023 | 318,009,023 |
| Percentages used | 33% / 13% | 33% / 13% |

Twelve account reads on one connection moved nothing. And the instrument works, which is the half that makes the zero mean something — the same counter moved by 29,188,602 across a day of ordinary use. A counter that *cannot* move is indistinguishable from one that did not.

Claude Code's side is the same kind of read: Lancetta asks your Claude account how much is left, with the sign-in Claude Code already keeps on your Mac — the question Claude Code's own `/usage` asks. No model is in the loop, so nothing is billed.

**Asking a model how much quota is left is a turn, and it would cost tokens on every poll.** It is the easiest thing to build and the one route this app will never take. See [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads).

## An unknown is never a number

The defect that started this project was a display rendering an unknown as `0%`, and a three-hour-old reading as current. So:

- a window with no reading is drawn as *unknown*, never as zero;
- every reading carries the time it was taken;
- a source that has fallen behind its own cadence says so, because a frozen number is itself an event.

## Requirements

- macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later
- At least one of **Codex** or **Claude Code** installed. Lancetta finds them itself; nothing has to be configured by hand.
- The notch panel needs a Mac that has a notch. On every other Mac that pane is hidden entirely and the menu-bar item behaves identically.
