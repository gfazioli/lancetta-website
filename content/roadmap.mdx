# What’s next

Nothing here is a promise, and nothing ahead is numbered or dated: the order changes as the work teaches you things (two of the entries on this page were built early, and one was deleted by a measurement). What is worth publishing is the other half: **what each step has to do before it counts as finished**. The versions that have shipped are at the end of the page, named, because they exist and you can download them.

## It speaks first

A percentage on an icon is passive: it rewards looking. The value is in the two moments the app can speak first.

**Alarms** — a window refused you, a threshold was crossed, or a source has gone quiet past its own cadence. A frozen number is the original defect of this whole project, so its silence is itself an event.

**Advice** — nothing changed, but the shape of the next hour did. *"The 5-hour window resets in 12 minutes and you are at 33% — the remaining 67% goes with it."*

That sentence is only true if a window is a bucket that empties at an instant. If quota instead drains back gradually, the advice is wrong in the one direction that costs you money: it tells you to spend. So the advice half was made conditional on a measurement, with the rule fixed in advance — a step function and it ships, a slope and the feature is deleted rather than softened.

**Measured on 20 September 2026, and it came back a step.** 1,890 readings, both agents, both windows: across twenty-five windows the floor of the series never fell, and every boundary with something left to lose dropped in one step — the sharpest of them from 100% to zero in forty-two seconds. So the advice is a true sentence.

**The advice half has shipped**, as the [pace line](/docs/the-menu#the-pace-line) under each bar. The alarms half has not: that is what is still next here.

Two things that measurement did not settle, and the app now carries the answer rather than the assumption: one agent's weekly window was never seen to reset during the sample, and the other crossed exactly one boundary. Advice about a window whose reset nobody has watched is not advice — so the weekly line stays silent on your Mac until your Mac has watched that window turn over at least once. It is a fact about your account, not a verdict shipped from ours.

## Everyone else's Mac

The discovery UI and the Sources pane. This is the version where the app stops assuming one machine, and it is a bigger step than it looks. Connecting Claude is already one click and a macOS dialog, not a script.

Half of this arrived early: the app already ships signed, notarized and downloadable, so the updater in it has a feed with something at the other end.

## Reset credits

Redeeming a Codex reset credit from the panel, with every safety rule an irreversible action needs.

## Not planned

**Asking a model how much quota is left.** It is the easiest source to write and it costs tokens on every poll. It is the one route this app will never take — see [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads).

**Routing around a limit.** No proxy in the path, no failing over to a second account, and nothing firing a token at a schedule to keep a session warm. Those are real things real tools do, and wanting them is reasonable — but they are a different product. This one rests entirely on the claim that the number in front of you is the truth about your account, and a tool that spends quota to hold a connection open, or moves you to another account when the first one refuses, has quietly made that number mean something else. Lancetta tells you where you stand and gets out of the way.

## Already shipped

### v0.4 — the pace, and the panel

*Shipped 2026-09-21.*

**The pace line.** Under each bar, one line saying where that window ends at the rate you are going, from the series Lancetta keeps of its own readings. It is always there and turns amber only when the window would run out before it resets, because a line that appears only in trouble is one nobody has learnt to read by the time it matters. The weekly line waits until your Mac has watched that window turn over — see above for why.

**The menu became a panel.** A macOS menu's background belongs to the system, so the one surface of the app that was system grey could not be anything else while it stayed a menu. It is now Lancetta's own navy, one card per agent, and 318 points wide where the menu needed 466 for the same content — a menu reserves a column for keyboard shortcuts that nothing can reclaim. Escape, ⌘R, ⌘O, ⌘, and ⌘Q all still work; Settings, Check for Updates, About and Quit are buttons along the bottom rather than four more rows.

### v0.3 — Claude from the account

*Shipped 2026-09-18.*

The Claude card used to ask for a line in your status-line script, and the first install on a second Mac showed what that looks like to anyone else: a card pointing at instructions that lived on this site. Now it is one click and one macOS dialog. Lancetta asks your Claude account how much is left, with the sign-in Claude Code already keeps on your Mac — the question Claude Code's own `/usage` asks, no model in the loop — so Claude refreshes on demand the way Codex does. The status-line route stays for anyone who would rather not hand the app a keychain item. [Getting Started](/docs/getting-started#claude-code) says exactly what the click does and does not do.

Two smaller things rode along: **What's New** under Help, with the release's own notes inside the app; and the app icon in the format macOS 26 introduced, so the Dock stops drawing a white plate around it.

### v0.2 — the quota, and rather more than the quota

*Shipped 2026-09-18 — the first release.*

The point of this version was narrow and concrete: stop reading a quota number off a terminal status line. That cannot be done with one agent, because the status line shows both — so both quota cards ship together or the first version does not achieve the one thing it exists for.

- Codex quota, with binary discovery that works from a Finder launch (where a GUI app inherits almost no `PATH`)
- Claude Code quota, from the status-line payload, with the card honest about its age when nothing has rendered
- The menu-bar item: alarm first, percentage second, age visible when a source falls behind, and configurable per agent
- Per-agent identity — a colour and the vendor's own mark, carried by the agent rather than hardcoded in the menu, so a third agent is one new file
- The order the agents take, set once and obeyed by every surface
- The island under the notch
- Settings, About, and a Check-for-Updates that says what state the build is in

**Done when** it runs launched from the Finder — not from a terminal that has a `PATH` — both cards agree with what each tool reports directly, and a working day passes without anyone looking at the status line.

**Two things arrived early and are in this version rather than the next two.**

**The processes they leave behind** — the reaper: count, total footprint, orphan count, and a **Reclaim** that lists what it will stop *before* stopping it, in the panel and in the window. It was planned for a later version and is built. It reproduces the twelve-of-fourteen result with zero live trees touched, and it is driven against decoy processes in an automated self-test rather than against the machine it is running on. See [The processes they leave behind](/docs/memory).

**The window** — Overview, Usage, Limits and Processes, with the daily token series drawn as a chart. The series was planned for much later and turned out to be free: Codex already keeps it and gives it away. See [The Window](/docs/the-window).

And with them, three things that came along with turning Lancetta into a real application bundle rather than a bare executable: **open at login**, a switch to **keep the icon in the Dock**, and the **updater** itself — the framework, the three controls and the feed address. The feed has had something at the other end since the day this shipped.
