# What’s next

Nothing here is a promise, and nothing ahead is numbered or dated: the order changes as the work teaches you things (two of the entries on this page were built early, and one was deleted by a measurement). What is worth publishing is the other half: **what each step has to do before it counts as finished**. The versions that have shipped are at the end of the page, named, because they exist and you can download them.

## Advice from your own history

Today the pace line reasons from one window: what you have spent since it opened, carried forward at that rate. It is honest arithmetic and it has a blind spot — it does not know that you start at nine, that Thursday is your long day, or that you never touch it at the weekend. So it can tell you where *this* afternoon lands and not what a normal one costs you.

The readings are already being kept. The step is reasoning across them instead of inside one window: a working-hours setting, so the app knows which hours are yours; an average across your own days; and then the two sentences that need both — **when to start**, and **when you will probably stop**.

**Done when** it can say, on a Monday morning and before you have spent anything, roughly how far into the day the five-hour window will carry you — and be right often enough that the sentence is worth reading. That is a higher bar than the pace line had to clear, because a projection made before there is any evidence is a forecast rather than arithmetic, and a forecast that is wrong twice stops being read. If it cannot clear it, it says nothing, the way every other case that cannot support a projection already does.

## Everyone else's Mac

The discovery UI and the Sources pane. This is the version where the app stops assuming one machine, and it is a bigger step than it looks. Connecting Claude is already one click and a macOS dialog, not a script.

Half of this arrived early: the app already ships signed, notarized and downloadable, so the updater in it has a feed with something at the other end.

## Not planned

**Asking a model how much quota is left.** It is the easiest source to write and it costs tokens on every poll. It is the one route this app will never take — see [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads).

**Routing around a limit.** No proxy in the path, no failing over to a second account, and nothing firing a token at a schedule to keep a session warm. Those are real things real tools do, and wanting them is reasonable — but they are a different product. This one rests entirely on the claim that the number in front of you is the truth about your account, and a tool that spends quota to hold a connection open, or moves you to another account when the first one refuses, has quietly made that number mean something else. Lancetta tells you where you stand and gets out of the way.

## Already shipped

### v0.12 — the week that just ended

*Shipped on release.*

**A look back at last week.** The readings Lancetta keeps finally answer a question: under the side-by-side, the week that just ended, per agent — when each weekly window ran out and when it was back, how often the five-hour one stopped you and for how long, and how much of the week nobody read. A night the Mac spent asleep is a gap, never a zero, and until your Mac has watched a whole week it says so instead of showing part of one. See [Last week](/docs/the-window#last-week).

**Both agents side by side.** The Overview shows every agent's two windows on one row, each with its reset and where it is heading — and never which agent to use. See [Overview](/docs/the-window#overview).

### v0.11 — a free reset, in hand

*Shipped 2026-09-23.*

**Codex grants free resets, and nothing showed them.** The count arrived with every reading and went nowhere. Now the card says *1 free reset · until 22 Oct*, and **Use…** spends one — after a question that puts two dates side by side: when each window would come back on its own, and when the credit lapses, with Cancel as the default. A spent window with a credit in hand stops claiming there is *nothing more until it resets*, a retry after a lost answer can never spend a second credit, and a new grant is a notification — even one that landed while Lancetta was closed. See [A free reset](/docs/the-menu#a-free-reset).

**And 0.11.1: no more Reconnect.** Claude Code rewrites its sign-in every time it renews it, and each rewrite used to take Lancetta's permission with it, so the Claude card kept asking to be reconnected. Lancetta now reads the sign-in the way Claude Code itself does: a renewal costs nothing, and there is normally no dialog at all, not even the first time.

### v0.10 — the panel's own toolbar

*Shipped 2026-09-23.*

The top of the panel says how fresh the numbers are, and clicking it refreshes them without closing the panel. Settings sits beside the pin, the foot is down to Open Lancetta and Quit, a right-click on the menu-bar item gives the classic menu of every command, and an update shows up as a button only when there is one. The panel has one clock: a card adds its own age only when it trails that line.

### v0.9 — a pin, and times the way you say them

*Shipped 2026-09-22.*

A pin keeps the panel on screen and in front while you work elsewhere. And every length of time follows one rule — two units, the larger first — so an hour and four minutes stopped printing as `64m`, and a day and eight minutes as `24h08m`.

### v0.8 — a lamp in the menu bar

*Shipped 2026-09-22.*

A bead beside each agent in the menu bar: green while the window is yours, amber past halfway, red near the end, black with a red edge when nothing is left. It is always there, where the sign it replaced only appeared once you were already blocked.

### v0.7 — the model's week, under the notch too

*Shipped 2026-09-22.*

Open the island and a model's own weekly window is drawn there as well, beside the five-hour and seven-day bars.

### v0.6 — the window one model keeps to itself

*Shipped 2026-09-22.*

**Some plans limit one model separately, and every monitor was reading past it.** The account's answer carries a row per meter, and the two windows everyone reads are only the first two of them. Lancetta now draws the rest: a bar per model that has a weekly limit of its own, under the name the account gives it, on the card and in the window, with the same pace line beneath. It says so too — when that week is spent, when it is back, and when it is heading for empty ahead of its reset — without ever calling you blocked, because your agent carries on with another model. See [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads#a-window-one-model-keeps-to-itself).

### v0.5 — it speaks first

*Shipped 2026-09-22.*

**The alarms half.** A percentage on an icon is passive: it rewards looking. Lancetta now speaks first, and only about what the menu bar cannot show: a window that will run out before it resets, with the reset named beside the moment it runs out; an agent with nothing left; one that is ready again; a reading that has stopped moving. Each once, at the moment it changes, never on launch, and with a sound only for the two moments you are not looking at a screen. Alerts and suggestions are separate switches, each agent has its own, and macOS is asked for permission the first time there is something to say. See [Alerts](/docs/alerts).

**Two things two Macs taught.** Refresh now no longer puts the login-keychain dialog up — that dialog belongs to Connect alone, and a renewed sign-in is a card offering Reconnect. And a Claude card still read through the status-line file now says so, and offers the account beside the number: that file moves only while a Claude Code session is open, which is why Refresh now could not move it.

### v0.4 — the pace, and the panel

*Shipped 2026-09-21.*

**The pace line.** Under each bar, one line saying where that window ends at the rate you are going, from the series Lancetta keeps of its own readings. That sentence is only true if a window is a bucket that empties at an instant; if quota drained back gradually the advice would be wrong in the one direction that costs you money, so it was made conditional on a measurement with the rule fixed in advance — a step and it ships, a slope and it is deleted rather than softened. **Measured on 20 September 2026, and it came back a step:** 1,890 readings, both agents, both windows; across twenty-five windows the floor of the series never fell, and every boundary with something left to lose dropped in one step, the sharpest from 100% to zero in forty-two seconds. The line is always there and turns amber only when the window would run out before it resets, because a line that appears only in trouble is one nobody has learnt to read by the time it matters. The weekly line waits until your Mac has watched that window turn over at least once — advice about a reset nobody has watched is not advice, and it is a fact about your account rather than a verdict shipped from ours.

**The menu became a panel.** A macOS menu's background belongs to the system, so the one surface of the app that was system grey could not be anything else while it stayed a menu. It is now Lancetta's own navy, one card per agent, and 380 points wide where the menu needed 466 for the same content — a menu reserves a column for keyboard shortcuts that nothing can reclaim. Escape, ⌘R, ⌘O, ⌘, and ⌘Q all still work; Settings, Check for Updates, About and Quit are buttons along the bottom rather than four more rows.

### v0.3 — Claude from the account

*Shipped 2026-09-18.*

The Claude card used to ask for a line in your status-line script, and the first install on a second Mac showed what that looks like to anyone else: a card pointing at instructions that lived on this site. Now it is one click and one macOS dialog. Lancetta asks your Claude account how much is left, with the sign-in Claude Code already keeps on your Mac — the question Claude Code's own `/usage` asks, no model in the loop — so Claude refreshes on demand the way Codex does. The status-line route stays for anyone who would rather not hand the app a keychain item. [Getting Started](/docs/getting-started#claude-code) says exactly what the click does and does not do.

Two smaller things rode along: **What's New** under Help, with the release's own notes inside the app; and the app icon in the format macOS 26 introduced, so the Dock stops drawing a white plate around it.

### v0.2 — the quota, and rather more than the quota

*Shipped 2026-09-18 — the first release.*

The point of this version was narrow and concrete: stop reading a quota number off a terminal status line. That cannot be done with one agent, because the status line shows both — so both quota cards ship together or the first version does not achieve the one thing it exists for.

- Codex quota, with binary discovery that works from a Finder launch (where a GUI app inherits almost no `PATH`)
- Claude Code quota, from the status-line payload, with the card honest about its age when nothing has rendered
- The menu-bar item: alarm first, percentage second, age visible when a source falls behind, and configurable per agent
- Per-agent identity — a colour and the vendor's own mark, carried by the agent rather than hardcoded in the menu, so a third agent is one new file
- The order the agents take, set once and obeyed by every surface
- The island under the notch
- Settings, About, and a Check-for-Updates that says what state the build is in

**Done when** it runs launched from the Finder — not from a terminal that has a `PATH` — both cards agree with what each tool reports directly, and a working day passes without anyone looking at the status line.

**Two things arrived early and are in this version rather than the next two.**

**The processes they leave behind** — the reaper: count, total footprint, orphan count, and a **Reclaim** that lists what it will stop *before* stopping it, in the panel and in the window. It was planned for a later version and is built. It reproduces the twelve-of-fourteen result with zero live trees touched, and it is driven against decoy processes in an automated self-test rather than against the machine it is running on. See [The processes they leave behind](/docs/memory).

**The window** — Overview, Usage, Limits and Processes, with the daily token series drawn as a chart. The series was planned for much later and turned out to be free: Codex already keeps it and gives it away. See [The Window](/docs/the-window).

And with them, three things that came along with turning Lancetta into a real application bundle rather than a bare executable: **open at login**, a switch to **keep the icon in the Dock**, and the **updater** itself — the framework, the three controls and the feed address. The feed has had something at the other end since the day this shipped.
