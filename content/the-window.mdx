# The Window

The panel gives you the three most urgent suggestions and the numbers in brief. The window has room for all of it: **every suggestion, open**, and then what each one rests on. It shows how much has been spent over weeks, what each window is doing in detail, what the agents have left running, what they read before they start, and which of your Claude Code and Codex sessions wait on you.

**Open Lancetta…** (⌘O) from the menu bar.

![The Lancetta window on its Overview: four suggestions first (Claude stops in 51m at this pace, Codex has room, Maintenance found 4 warnings, 3 orphaned Codex trees hold 1.05 GB), then the cards for Usage, Limits, Processes and Maintenance, with Usage open on the week’s tokens](/screenshot-window-overview.png)

Six panes, in the sidebar: **Overview**, **Usage**, **Limits**, **Processes**, **Maintenance** and **Sessions**. Each pane is a row of cards: a card shows one figure and a line under it, and **a click opens its details under it**, joined to it, while a second click closes them. One card is open at a time in each pane, and each pane remembers which one.

The window opens where you left it, at the size you left it. The first time, it opens at 1200 by 800 points. It is a normal window and it closes like one. Closing it quits nothing, and the menu-bar item is always the way back.

**A Dock icon appears while it is open**, and goes again when you close it. That is not an oversight: a window needs its app to be a normal one to get a menu bar of its own and to come forward when you ask for it, and a normal app has a Dock icon. At rest, with no window open, Lancetta is back to being an accessory with nothing in the Dock. If you would rather it stayed in the Dock the whole time, [one switch](/docs/settings#general) does that.

## Overview

**Every suggestion first, all of them open**, in the order the panel uses: the most urgent first. What each one is and when it appears is on [Suggestions](/docs/suggestions). With nothing to suggest, one line says so, and says whether the readings show room in every window.

Under them, a card for each of the other panes, with what that pane would tell you first:

- **Usage**: the tokens of the last seven days. Opened, the week's chart, one bar per agent per day, with a chooser for either agent alone or both.
- **Limits**: the fullest window of any agent, and when it resets. Opened, **Quota Used**, every agent on one row with its 5-hour and 7-day windows side by side: the figure, the bar, the time to the reset and, when there is something worth saying, when your recent rate would run the window out. A model with a weekly limit of its own is drawn under its agent. The title follows the setting that decides what the bars are filled with, so it reads *Quota Left* when you are counting down. Under it is **Last week**.
- **Processes**: how many background trees are running, and what they hold, or, when some are orphaned, what the orphans hold.
- **Maintenance**: how many warnings the scan found, and how much the heaviest start loads.
- **Sessions**: how many of your Claude Code and Codex sessions wait on you, and how many are working. Opened, those sessions, each with Open and Done.

The arrow in a card's corner opens that pane.

### Last week

Open the **Limits** card on the Overview, and under Quota Used is the week that just ended.

Lancetta keeps a record of the quota it reads — each change, and a line every fifteen minutes while nothing moves — so it can tell you what the week that just ended did. Each agent's own **weekly window**, not a calendar week: Claude's and Codex's turn over on different days, so each agent's lines say which days they cover.

- **Weekly** — when it ran out and when it was back (*Spent Thursday 24 at 14:10, back Saturday 26 at 09:00*), or the highest reading of it — and, if the provider emptied the window before its reset, when. If Lancetta was not reading at the moment something happened, you get the range it happened in, never a guess at a minute inside it.
- **5-hour** — how many 5-hour windows reached 100%, and how long they held you up in all, counted to each window's reset.
- **Not seen** — how much of the week had no reading at all: the Mac asleep, the app quit, or the agent not answering. Time nobody read is said as such and never counted as a quiet hour.
- One line against **the week before**, once there is a whole one to compare with.

It needs one whole week. Until Lancetta has read a week from its start, the card says when the first whole one ends rather than passing a few days off as a week. Codex's week begins with your first request after a reset, so a Codex you have not touched shows *No quota used from … to …* — and only when Lancetta read it often enough to know, up to the last time it did. It is a card and not a notification on purpose: nothing in a look back is urgent.

## Usage

![The Usage pane: three cards (the tokens of the last 30 days, the fullest 5-hour window at 100% and how many days it ran out, Codex’s lifetime total), and Total Usage open under them: thirty days of tokens for both agents side by side](/screenshot-window-usage.png)

Three cards:

- **Total Usage**: the tokens of the range, and, opened, the chart over **7, 30 or 90 days**, with the totals underneath.
- **Fullest 5-Hour Window**: the fullest any agent's 5-hour window got in the range, and on how many days one ran out.
- **Codex**: what Codex keeps of its whole history: lifetime tokens, the best single day, how many days have been recorded at all, the current and longest streaks, and the longest single turn. It is there only when Codex has a history to give.

{/* The Fullest 5-Hour Window picture (`/screenshot-fullest-window.png`) showed the
    0.16 card on its own. Since 0.50 it opens from a card of the Usage pane, and the
    picture is to be re-shot with that card open. */}

**The fullest 5-hour window of each day.** Open its card and, for every day in the range, how full each agent's fullest 5-hour window got, drawn against the whole window: the faint column behind each bar is 100%. Above the chart, each agent's fullest day in the range and how many days a window ran out. It comes from the readings Lancetta keeps on this Mac rather than from either agent, so a day starts at four in the morning — a window opened at half past midnight is the evening before carrying on — and a day with no column had no window used, or Lancetta was not running: the readings cannot tell the two apart, and the caption says so. With [Count down what is left](/docs/settings#general) on, the card shows the least that was left instead. It says what happened; what to do about the hours you usually work is still to come, on [the roadmap](/docs/roadmap#advice-from-your-own-history).

Two things about the token chart are worth knowing, because both would otherwise read as bugs.

**The two series do not come from the same place, and the caption says so.** Codex keeps the whole token history and gives it away free — one bucket per day used, and the buckets sum to the lifetime figure exactly, so it is the complete series rather than a window onto it. **Claude Code publishes no counterpart at all**: its payload carries two percentages and two reset instants and nothing else. So Claude's column is rebuilt from Claude Code's own transcripts on your Mac, which is honest about two limits the caption states — sessions on another machine are not in it, and cache reads are left out, because they re-send the same context every turn and would be 98% of the total.

**The last column is still growing.** Codex publishes a day's tokens with a lag of hours, not of a day — on the afternoon this was measured the newest bucket was still yesterday's, and by the evening today's had appeared. So the caption under the chart says which of those two states you are looking at, rather than asserting one of them. A day with no usage is drawn as a gap, never as a zero: the series only contains days that were used, and inventing the quiet ones would compress months into weeks.

The percentage beside the total compares **the last 7, 30 or 90 complete days with as many days before them**. Today is left out of both, being still under way, and the days are calendar days, from midnight. That is the only comparison the buckets support without inventing a baseline.

### What a total covers

A total is a claim about every agent it counts, so it starts from the agents you asked for: the ones watched in [Settings › Agents](/docs/settings#agents), and of those the ones the chooser shows. With every one of them read, the total is whole, *tokens · Codex + Claude*, and compared with the days before. With one of them not read, the total is the sum of the others and says so: *tokens · Claude only* under the figure, *partial: Codex not read* in amber on its card, and a line under the chart with the code and what went wrong. **A partial total is never compared**, since a read that failed would pass for a drop. While an agent is still loading, the card says *partial: Codex still loading*; with none read, the figure is a dash and the line under it says why.

An agent with no history is not a missing one: a Codex with no day published yet, or a Mac with no Claude Code transcripts, adds nothing and leaves the total whole.

- **Codex** publishes each day hours late, so every day up to yesterday counts as published, quiet or not, and today counts once Codex has published it. When its last read failed, the **Codex** card keeps the figures it last had and says *Codex not read*, in amber.
- **Claude**'s tokens are counted from Claude Code's transcripts. When that folder, or a folder in it, cannot be read, Claude is not counted rather than its last count shown as current: that is **CLAUDE-14** on [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting).

## Limits

![The Limits pane: a card per agent, Claude at 82% and Codex at 20%, and Claude’s open under them: its 5-hour window with the pace line under it (at this rate it runs out in 50m, before the reset), its 7-day window, and the Fable model’s own weekly limit, counted separately](/screenshot-window-limits.png)

A card per agent, with the fuller of its two windows and when it resets. Opened, the agent in full, and this is where the asymmetry between the two is on screen rather than in a footnote:

- **Codex** is marked **live** — it can be asked, so the reading is as current as the last poll.
- **Claude** is marked **seen *n* ago** whenever its reading is not fresh: through the status-line file always, because that reading cannot be asked for; through the account only while the account is asking for a pause and the card is holding the last reading it got. Either way the card shows the age instead of pretending to a freshness it does not have.

Under that, both windows: the percentage, the bar, when it rolls over, and the [pace line](/docs/the-menu#the-pace-line) under the window it is about. A window with no reading shows `—`, never `0%`. See [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads).

When Codex holds a free reset, the same row the panel draws sits at the foot of its card — *1 free reset · until 29 Oct*, with **Use…** — and the notification about a new one opens here. See [A free reset](/docs/the-menu#a-free-reset).

Under those, when your account keeps one, **its own weekly limit, counted separately** — a bar per model that has one, under the name the account gives it, with the same pace line beneath. The heading is there because the bar alone invites the wrong reading: it is not a slice of the weekly window above it, it is a limit of its own, and it can be spent while that one is at two thirds.

### Where each window is heading

At the foot of an agent's card, under everything you can act on, Limits draws both windows, whatever [the panel](/docs/the-menu#where-each-window-is-heading) is set to draw: the panel is for a glance, and this is where you come to see what a suggestion rests on.

**7 days**, from the week's opening to its reset. What went so far, with the nights shaded from 22:00 to 07:00, and behind it the 5-hour windows the week was made of, as thin teeth on the same scale: how full each session got, beside how far the week moved. Last week is dotted over this one: found by its own reset and lined up from its own opening, so each point is where last week stood at the same point of its window, even when days went by between its reset and the start of this one. The week carries no line forward, because a week projected from one afternoon would be a forecast and Lancetta makes none. The one exception is a week the pace line says will stop you, which gets an amber line to that moment.

**5 hours**, hour by hour, beside a clock. What went, and from the newest reading a cone of where it is heading. Its dashed middle is the last hour's rate, the one the pace line and the suggestions rest on, labelled *at this rate* with where it leaves the window at the reset. Its edges are the slowest and fastest of the rate over half an hour, an hour and two hours, so a wide cone says how far to trust the middle without a sentence. When that rate empties the window before its reset, the middle turns amber, the moment is named in clock time on the limit line, and the stretch from there to the reset is hatched: the time you would wait. The clock is the panel's dial at full size.

Amber means here exactly what the pace line means by *at this rate you run out*, on the window that would stop you first, and nothing else: not a rate that brushes 100% while the line stays quiet, and not spending ahead of an even pace.

**A stretch nobody read is never drawn flat.** When the readings stop, with the Mac asleep or Lancetta quit, the line does not hold the old figure across the gap as if nothing had been spent: a fainter dashed connector runs straight from the reading before to the reading after, because the figure got from one to the other somewhere in there, and that is all anyone knows. The teeth and last week are drawn the same way. A window starts from zero at its opening only when this Mac was reading at that moment; otherwise its line starts at the first reading.

Each chart's legend names only the lines it draws. With [Count down what is left](/docs/settings#general) on, the charts plot what is left, falling toward the limit at 0%.

## Processes

![The Processes pane: 4 Codex trees holding 1.45 GB, 3 of them orphaned holding 1.05 GB, and the list open under them: each tree with the directory it was started for, what it holds, its children and its pid, the three orphans marked with a warning](/screenshot-window-processes.png)

Two cards: **Codex trees**, every Codex background tree on the machine and what they hold between them, and **Orphaned**, the ones nothing can reach. Open the first for the whole list: each tree with the directory it was started for, what it is holding, its children and its pid. Open the second for the orphans alone, with what they hold and the **Reclaim…** button.

A green tick is a tree something can still reach. An orange triangle is an **orphan** — its working directory is gone, so nothing will ever shut it down, and it will hold that memory until the Mac is restarted. **Reclaim…** is only live when there is something to reclaim, and it lists what it will close before closing anything. While there are orphans, a suggestion says so at the top of the Overview, with the same button. The full story is on [The processes they leave behind](/docs/memory).

The pane counts a tree's whole descent rather than the three processes its own documentation describes: `invoice-parser` above has four children, and a real one was measured holding eleven.

## Maintenance

![The Maintenance pane: 4 warnings and 5 suggestions across 18 files in 4 places, Fix All and Copy Findings at the top, the heaviest start loading 6,983 of the 120,000 characters at which Claude Code warns, and the Instructions card open on the files that need fixing: a long CLAUDE.md, an AGENTS.md that Claude Code skips, a personal file committed to git, and a CLAUDE.md with a suggestion](/screenshot-window-maintenance.png)

What your coding agents read before they start, and what it costs: CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, rules, skills, commands, settings and memory, in every repository Claude Code or Codex has worked in recently and the folders above it. Each finding says why it matters and how to fix it. A fix that needs no choosing is shown in full before it is applied, one at a time or all at once, and the rest opens in Claude Code or Codex. Everything this pane checks, and what it changes, is on [Maintenance](/docs/maintenance).

## Sessions

![The Sessions pane: 3 on Your turn, the longest waiting for 2h05m, 2 Working, 1 Concluded today and 3 in the last 7 days; Your turn open on its three sessions as cards, each with its mark, name and folder, where it stands and for how long (Your turn, Asks you something, Stopped before it was done), when it started, how its time split between working and waiting, and Open and Done](/screenshot-window-sessions.png)

Every Claude Code and Codex session you started, as a post-it on a wall: **Your turn**, the sessions waiting on you, with how long the longest has waited; **Working**; and **Concluded**, what you called done or removed, day by day. Each session opens in your terminal from here, and goes down with **Done** without being closed. The whole pane is on [Sessions](/docs/sessions).

## Pull to refresh

On macOS 27 you can pull any pane down to refresh it, the way you would in Mail. Each pane reads again what it shows:

| Pane | What a pull reads again |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | the quotas and the process trees, as **Refresh now** does, then the token history and Last week |
| **Usage** | the token history, with Codex asked again, and the 5-hour windows |
| **Limits** | the quotas and the process trees, as **Refresh now** does |
| **Processes** | the process trees, and nothing else: asking the agents from there would spend their reads on numbers that are not on screen |
| **Maintenance** | where Claude Code and Codex have worked recently, and every agent file there |
| **Sessions** | the sessions Claude Code and Codex each keep |

The spinner holds for a couple of seconds at most and lets go; the reading carries on underneath, and the cards it reads again press as the numbers land. A second pull while the first refresh is still out starts nothing new: it waits for that one rather than throwing its answers away. The sidebar is for choosing a pane, so pulling it does nothing. On any version of macOS, **Refresh now** (⌘R), in the panel or in the window's Agents menu, still reads the quotas and the trees.

## Why the window is dark

Because it always is. The window has one appearance rather than following the system, the same call Netfox makes — the surface is a dense instrument panel of bars and charts, and the palette is part of reading it. The panel is always dark for the same reason. Only the item in the menu bar follows the system appearance, because it has to belong among the other items there.
