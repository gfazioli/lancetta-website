# Why Lancetta

There are good tools in this space, and one of them is free, open source and watches
seventy-four providers. If you have read this far you deserve a straight answer about
where Lancetta fits, so here it is — including the cases where you should use something
else.

## Use CodexBar instead if you pay for a lot of things

[CodexBar](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar) is very good, and the comparison starts
there rather than ending there. It covers Codex, Claude, Cursor, Copilot, Gemini,
OpenRouter, Bedrock and around seventy more, it is MIT licensed, and it ships almost
daily.

If your bill has five names on it, that breadth is the whole product and nothing here
replaces it. Lancetta watches **two** agents — Codex and Claude Code — and has no plan to
watch seventy.

What follows is what Lancetta does with those two that breadth cannot buy.

## What a tool has to know before it can tell you anything useful

Three separate things, and almost nobody holds all of them.

- **The ceiling** — how much of your quota is actually left, as the provider reports it.
- **The flow** — how many tokens you have really spent, read from your own transcripts.
- **The series** — what the quota did over the past days, kept rather than recomputed.

| | ceiling | flow | series |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ccusage** | guessed | yes | no |
| **CodexBar** | yes | yes | no |
| **Quotio** | yes | partly | no |
| **Lancetta** | yes | yes | **yes** |

**ccusage** reads the transcripts on your disk, so it knows exactly how many tokens went
out and has no way at all to learn what your limit is. Its projections run against a
ceiling you have to supply or it infers from your biggest previous session. That is an
honest tool doing the best it can with half the information.

**CodexBar** has the ceiling and the flow, and keeps no memory of either. Its burn-down is
computed from the reading in front of it: how much of the window has elapsed against how
much you have used. Nothing is stored. With seventy-four providers that is the only
sane choice — one saved series per provider per window is a commitment multiplied by
seventy-four.

**Quotio** answers a limit by routing around it: a local proxy that fails over to another
account, and a scheduler that fires small requests to keep sessions warm. A different
philosophy, and a legitimate one. It is the opposite of this app's founding rule.

**Lancetta has been recording since v0.2.** Every reading, both agents, both windows, in a
plain file on your Mac. That is the only thing on this page that gets harder to copy the
longer it runs.

## So what does that buy you

**A number you can date.** Every reading on every surface carries its own age. When a
source falls behind, it says so instead of going quiet — because the defect that started
this project was a status line showing a three-hour-old number as if it were current, and
an unknown drawn as `0%`.

**The bucket that refused, named.** When a limit stops you, you see *which* window stopped
you. Not an average across all of them that lands somewhere comfortable and tells you
nothing.

**Every failure has a code.** Eighteen of them, each with a sentence saying what happened,
why, and what to do — the same list the app shows you, this site's
[troubleshooting page](/docs/troubleshooting), and any bug report you ever file. A tool
that can only say "unavailable" leaves you with no words to describe what went wrong.

**The processes nobody else reaps.** Both agents leave a background tree behind for every
folder they worked in, and the ones whose folder is gone can never be reconnected to. On
one Mac: twenty-eight of them, holding 2.68 GB. No other tool in this comparison looks for
them at all. [How that works](/docs/memory).

**And reading costs nothing.** No model is asked anything, ever. Measured, not assumed:
twelve account reads left the lifetime token counter unmoved.

## What is coming, and why only this app can say it

A five-hour refusal costs you a coffee break. Running out of your **weekly** window on a
Tuesday costs three working days — and by the time that bar is red it is far too late to
do anything about it.

A sentence like *"at this week's pace your weekly window runs out Thursday afternoon"* is
impossible from a single reading. It needs history, which is the one thing in the table
above that only Lancetta keeps. That is [what is next](/docs/roadmap).
