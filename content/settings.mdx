# Settings

⌘, from the panel, or the gear at its top. Six panes, and a search field that finds a setting by name when you cannot remember which pane it is in.

![Lancetta's Settings, on the General pane: the refresh interval and counting down what is left, the two advice switches, and the alert switches beneath them](/screenshot-settings-general.png)

## General

**Refresh every** — 15 seconds to 5 minutes, 45 by default. This is a **CPU budget, not a quota one**, and the difference is the whole point: reading a quota spends no tokens, so there is no cost pressure to poll slowly. What there is, is a process to spawn. See [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads).

It applies to Claude too once it is connected, with a floor: the account is asked at most once a minute whatever you set here, and Refresh now asks straight away. Through the status-line file instead, the Claude reading arrives when a session renders one, whatever the interval.

**Count down what is left** — off, the number is what has been *used* and rises from 0. On, a fresh window reads 100% and falls as tokens go, which is how Codex's own status line puts it.

Whichever you pick, the bars are filled with **the number that is shown**, and every label that names the quantity follows it. That sounds like a detail and is not: a card headed *Quota left* over a bar filled with what was used is the exact defect this app was built in response to, and the app has written it twice by accident since.

**Open the menu on hover** — off by default. On, pointing at Lancetta in the menu bar opens the panel after a quarter of a second, with no click; it closes when the pointer leaves it. Opening it this way deliberately **does not take the keyboard**, so nothing is pulled out of whatever you are typing in — which also means the panel's shortcuts are not live yet. Click the item and the panel is yours: it takes the keyboard, its shortcuts work, and it stays open until you click away, exactly as a clicked-open panel always did. Clicking a row inside it works either way.

The quarter-second is not a round number for its own sake: a pointer on its way to another app's menu-bar item crosses ours in a few dozen milliseconds, and a panel that opened on the way past would be in the way every time.

**Show where each window ends** — on by default. The [pace line](/docs/the-menu#the-pace-line): a caption under each bar saying how much of the window has gone, how long it has been open, and where that rate lands. It is an extrapolation of your own readings, not a prediction, and it says nothing at all until enough of the window has passed for a rate to mean anything.

**Only when a window would run out early** — off by default, and disabled while the line above is off. Off, the line is always there and turns amber when the window would empty before it resets. On, it appears only then. Always on is the default because a line that shows up only in trouble is one nobody has learnt to read by the time it matters.

### Alerts

The switches for what Lancetta says first, which is deliberately little: the percentage is already on your menu bar, so a notification is for what the bar cannot show. The whole of it is on [its own page](/docs/alerts); the controls are:

**Let Lancetta speak first** — on by default. macOS asks whether Lancetta may notify you the first time there is something worth saying, not now and not at launch.

**Alerts** — what the account reported: it refused you or a window is spent, it is ready again, or its reading has stopped moving.

**Suggestions** — what the series implies: a window heading for empty before it resets, with the reset named beside it. It follows the pace line above, because it is the same projection said louder.

**Sound when you are out** and **Sound when you can start again** — one of macOS's own sounds, or none. Only these two moments carry a sound: the work stopping, and the work being possible again.

**Also say when a window passes** — *Never* by default, because this one announces the number already in your menu bar. Kept for a hard threshold; each level is said once per window, going up, and again after the window resets.

**Open Lancetta at login** — off by default. It binds to macOS's own login-item status rather than to a copy of it, so the switch reflects what the system actually has registered.

**Show the support window at launch** — off by default. Opens About + Support when Lancetta starts. Netfox ships the same switch on; here it is off, because this app opens at login, and a window at every login is the thing a menu-bar app exists to avoid.

**Show the icon in the Dock** — off by default, which means Lancetta is an accessory app with nothing in the Dock. Note what that setting does *not* control: a Dock icon appears while [the window](/docs/the-window) is open whatever this is set to, because a window needs its app to be a normal one to have its own menu bar and to come forward when asked. This switch decides whether the icon stays there the rest of the time.

**Rotate agents every** — with more than one agent in the menu bar they take turns, cross-fading. The item holds a fixed width at the widest of them so its neighbours never shift. With one agent in the bar there is nothing to rotate, and the control is disabled rather than silently doing nothing.

## Appearance

![Lancetta's Settings, on the Appearance pane: one switch, Use the agents' own logos](/screenshot-settings-appearance.png)

**Marks** — the agents' own artwork, or neutral system symbols. The marks are drawn from vector path data rather than bundled as images, so they stay sharp at any row height.

## Notch

![Lancetta's Settings, on the Notch pane: one switch, Show the reading under the notch](/screenshot-settings-notch.png)

One switch, and the pane only exists on a Mac that has a notch. Hidden rather than shown dead: a pane whose feature can never run reads as broken. See [The Notch](/docs/the-notch).

## Agents

A list with a page per agent, the way System Settings does accounts and devices.

![Lancetta's Settings, on the Agents pane: Claude and Codex as two rows, each with a grip to drag it and a chevron to open it](/screenshot-settings-agents.png)

**The list is where the order is set** — drag a row and everything follows it: the menu, the window, the island, and the turn each agent takes in the menu bar. There is one sort, in one place, so no surface can have an order of its own. Before this existed Codex was always first, for no better reason than being first in an array; an order nobody chose is still an order somebody has to live with.

A row opens on a click anywhere, and drags from anywhere. The grip on the left says it can be dragged — it is the affordance, not the only handle.

**Each agent's page** carries:

- **Colour** — the tint its bars are drawn in, everywhere: the panel, the window, the island, and the token chart if the data is that agent's. A colour you set is a colour you expect to see. There is a Reset beside it.
- **Watch this agent** — off, it keeps no card in the menu and no cell in the bar. Lancetta refuses to turn off the last one: an empty menu is indistinguishable from an app that has stopped working, and the way back would be through the thing you just emptied.
- **Show in the menu bar** — separate from watching it, because an agent can be worth a card in the menu and not worth the bar's width. A refusal is shown regardless: when this agent is refused, its mark and a dead lamp take a turn in the bar even with this off, because the bar's rule is alarm first.
- **In the bar show** — its mark, its percentage, its reset, in any combination. The reset is off by default: it is four characters wide and the menu bar is the one place width is expensive. An agent set to show nothing still shows its mark, because an empty cell reads as a bug.
- **How it is wired** — which route that agent's numbers arrive by, so a card that is not updating tells you where to look. The panel says it too: a Claude card still read through the status-line file carries a line saying so, and a Connect button beside it.
- **Notify me about this agent** — off, Lancetta sends nothing about it: not a refusal, not a suggestion. The two agents are used differently and the answer differs with them; what the menu bar draws is unaffected.
- **Tell me when a free reset arrives** (Codex only) — a notification when Codex's account grants a reset credit, with what it resets and when it lapses; on by default. It lives here rather than under General because only Codex has credits, and a switch in General would read as a promise about every agent. See [A free reset](/docs/the-menu#a-free-reset).
- **Account** (Claude only) — **Connect** reads the sign-in Claude Code keeps on your Mac and asks your Claude account how much is left; macOS asks once whether to allow it. **Disconnect** stops it. What that click does and does not do is spelled out in [Getting Started](/docs/getting-started#claude-code).

![Claude's page in Lancetta's Settings: colour, Watch this agent, Show in the menu bar, the three bar fields, Notify me about this agent, and the Account section reading the quota from the Claude account, with a Disconnect button](/screenshot-settings-agent-claude.png)

The same page for Codex, where the line under the name says which executable it reads:

![Codex's page in Lancetta's Settings: the wiring line reads /opt/homebrew/bin/codex app-server on demand, then colour, Watch this agent, Show in the menu bar and the three bar fields, and under Notifications, Notify me about this agent and Tell me when a free reset arrives](/screenshot-settings-agent-codex.png)

Agents arrive **with the app**, not by being added by hand. A source is code that knows how to read one tool's quota *without spending any*, which is the whole constraint — there is nothing useful a text field could accept.

Visibility is stored as the set of agents you have **hidden**, rather than the set you have shown. An agent added in a later version is therefore absent from that set and is visible by default; stored the other way round, every new agent would ship invisible and read as broken until somebody found the setting. The same rule applies to the order: an agent the stored order has never heard of lands last, never nowhere.

## Updates

Three controls and the build you are running:

![Lancetta's Settings, on the Updates pane: Check for updates on launch, Automatically check for updates, the Check for Updates Now button, and the build stamp beside it](/screenshot-settings-updates.png)

- **Check for updates on launch** — off by default. A silent check when Lancetta starts, which speaks up only if there is something to say.
- **Automatically check for updates** — hands the schedule to the update framework, which then checks on its own cadence.
- **Check for Updates Now…** — the prompt, straight away.

**Both automatic checks are off until you turn them on, and nothing is checked in the meantime.** Every release, and what changed in it, is on the [release notes](/docs/release-notes) page.

## About

Version and build number, the sponsor link, and the trademark notice. It is reached from the header at the top of the sidebar, and also has its own window under **About Lancetta**, in the menu you get by right-clicking Lancetta in the menu bar.

![Lancetta's Settings, on the About pane: the icon, the version, the one-line description, the links, and the sponsors block](/screenshot-settings-about.png)

## The marks, and the trademark notice

The Codex and Claude marks are the vendors' own artwork, used **nominatively** — to name the products Lancetta reads. Lancetta is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, OpenAI or Anthropic. The marks are unmodified apart from colour, the notice is in About, and the switch under Appearance turns them off for anyone who would rather not display them.
