# Desktop widgets

The suggestion that matters and the windows it rests on, on the desktop, without opening the panel.

<LeadImage src={widgets} alt="Lancetta's three desktop widgets: a medium one with Claude and Codex side by side and, at its foot, the suggestion that at this pace Claude stops in 51 minutes; then a small one for Claude and a small one for Codex, each with its two windows and the time to each reset" />

## Adding them

Right-click the desktop › **Edit Widgets…**, search for **Lancetta** and drag the ones you want onto the desktop. Lancetta has to be running: the widgets draw what it reads, and they have nothing of their own to read.

- **Claude** and **Codex**, small: the agent's 5-hour and weekly windows, each as a figure, a bar and the time to its reset, and when they were read.
- **Your agents**, medium: every agent in the panel, side by side (the first two, in the panel's order), and the suggestion that matters most, with the time it was made beside it.

The figures say what is left or what is used, whichever the panel says ([Settings](/docs/settings)), and the bars are drawn in the colours you gave the agents. A click on any of them opens [Lancetta's window](/docs/the-window).

## Honest about time

The widgets show the panel's own readings. Lancetta writes them out for the widgets after it reads; nothing polls twice, and nothing else asks your account.

- **When it was read.** Every widget says when its numbers were read.
- **Past a reset, a dash.** When a window resets before Lancetta reads it again, the widget shows a dash and the time it reset, never the old number: the window has filled again by an amount nobody has read yet.
- **Over an hour old, faint.** The numbers turn faint and the time gets a warning clock: Lancetta was quit, or the Mac was asleep.
- **The countdowns move by themselves.** The figures change when Lancetta asks macOS to redraw the widgets, and macOS allows each widget a few dozen of those a day. Lancetta asks at once for news — an agent spent, refused, failing or back, a window that started again, a different suggestion — and otherwise when a figure has moved five points, or after half an hour. A suggestion's own words can hold a duration ("stops in 51m"), which is why its time stands beside it.

## Without colour

On the desktop, macOS drains a widget's colour whenever a window is in front of it. So nothing that matters is said by colour alone: a spent window says **Spent, back in 1h14m** beside an hourglass, a refusal says **Refused by the account** beside a raised hand, and an urgent suggestion has a warning sign.

## What they can read

Nothing but the one file Lancetta writes for them, in a folder the two share (see [Privacy](/docs/privacy#what-it-writes)). The widgets have no network access and run in macOS's sandbox: they cannot read your agents, your account or anything else on the Mac.
