# The Notch

On a MacBook Pro the reading also lives under the notch — the one piece of screen that was doing nothing.

![Lancetta's island collapsed under the notch: one bar per agent, Claude’s red because it stops within the hour, Codex’s in its own colour](/screenshot-notch-collapsed.png)

## Collapsed

Exactly as wide as the notch, and no wider. That is deliberate rather than tidy: a panel that overhangs the notch covers menu-bar items on either side, and a menu item you can see but cannot press is worse than no panel at all.

Collapsed, it shows **one thin bar per agent** — the five-hour window, in that agent's own colour, readable without moving the mouse. It is as tall as it needs to be for the agents you watch, so a third agent gets a third bar rather than being quietly cut off the bottom.

**A bar turns red when its agent has stopped, or will within the hour** at this pace — the same red the panel gives that [suggestion](/docs/suggestions#when-to-slow-down). Red rather than amber, because one of the agents' own colours is an orange, and amber on it would not read as an alarm. Its groove fills with the red too, so even a bar with nothing left in it says so.

## Expanded

![The Lancetta island open under the notch: a ring per agent carrying its mark and its five-hour reading, each window as a bar, and at the end the most urgent suggestion with its first step: at this pace Claude stops in 51m, then waits 1h18m for its reset; lower the effort, it is set to xhigh](/screenshot-notch-open.png)

Point at it and it **grows out of the notch**: a ring per agent carrying that agent's mark and its five-hour reading, its name, its plan, when the window resets, and then both windows as bars. Move away and it settles back.

**At the end, the advice.** When an agent has stopped, will stop, or will cost you later — a week running out early, a free reset about to lapse — the open island ends on the most urgent of those [suggestions](/docs/suggestions), in the panel's own words: its sentence and its first step. *"At this pace Claude stops in 51m, then waits 1h18m for its reset. Lower the effort: it is set to xhigh."* Room to spend stays out of the notch: the island is read at a glance, and a nudge there every five hours would be noise. The panel and [the window](/docs/the-window) carry every suggestion.

If your account keeps a **weekly window for one model**, it is drawn here too, under the two others and with the model's own name — the same row the panel shows. Open, the island is the panel's card in the notch, and a surface that draws the detail has no business hiding one of the windows: this is where you could otherwise read 68% while a model was already finished.

Collapsed it is deliberately absent, and for the same reason the menu bar does not carry it: that bar is the pressure of the next hour, comparable between agents, and a model running out does not stop the agent — it falls back to another model. Saying *spent* where the app means *about to be stopped* would be the one kind of wrong a quota monitor cannot afford.

Two things about how it behaves are on purpose:

**It is a "T", because the hardware is.** A column exactly the notch's width runs up to the top edge of the screen, and the wider body hangs underneath it. The physical cutout has rounded bottom corners, so an island with a straight top drawn from below the notch leaves two wedges of wallpaper showing between the two — it reads as detached however precisely it is positioned. This is the shape a phone's island has, for the same reason.

**Expanded, it is far wider than the notch — and the menu bar still works.** Everything that is not the island itself passes clicks straight through, including the notch's own column. You can see the island and press the menu-bar item behind its overhang.

**Every effect ends.** The rings, the bars and the digits reveal once with a stagger, a highlight crosses the bars once, and then nothing moves. A HUD that is on screen for the whole session cannot afford a perpetual animation; the workspace has already pinned a CPU core to a spinner that never stopped.

## On a Mac without a notch

There is no API that says whether a display has a notch — it is the *gap* between the two auxiliary areas of the menu bar, and a Mac without one looks identical until you measure it. Lancetta measures it, and on a Mac with no notch the island never appears and its section in Settings is hidden entirely.

One switch in [Settings](/docs/settings#notch) turns it off on a Mac that does have one. The setting survives moving between machines rather than being reset by whichever display you happened to be on.
