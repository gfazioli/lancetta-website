# Sessions

Run a few sessions at once, with Claude Code and with Codex, in terminals and in the background, and the hard part stops being the work: it is remembering **which one is waiting for you**. The **Sessions** pane of [the window](/docs/the-window) keeps every session you started as a post-it on a wall and says where each one stands, so the next one to pick up is the first one you see.

They are the agents' sessions, not Lancetta's: nothing here starts one or closes one. At every refresh Lancetta reads the list each agent keeps of its sessions, and changes nothing in either:

- **Claude Code**: the list Claude Code keeps of its sessions, in the background and in a terminal.
- **Codex**: the sessions open in Codex's shared background service, which Codex's terminal app runs every session through from Codex 0.163. Nothing is installed into Codex for it: no plugin, no hook, no line in its configuration. A `codex exec` run, a subagent's thread and a session Claude Code started in Codex are not sessions you sit at, and stay off the wall.

How each list is read, and what is not, is on [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads#your-sessions-for-the-wall). On a wall that holds both agents' sessions, each post-it carries its agent's mark in the corner of its badge.

## Four cards

- **Your turn**: the session finished its turn, asks you something, stopped or failed. The card says how long the one waiting longest has waited.
- **Working**: the agent is on it.
- **Concluded**: the sessions you called done, the ones you removed in Claude Code, and the ones that closed by themselves. The card counts today's, and under it the last seven days.
- **Saved**: what the agents still keep on disk once a session is off the wall, to open again or delete. See [Saved](#saved).

With both agents watched, a switch at the top shows **All**, **Claude** or **Codex**: every card follows it, and so does the sessions' own panel.

Each of the first three opens into its sessions, **as cards or as a list**: one switch for all three, kept from one launch to the next. A menu orders them: oldest first, newest first, by when they started, by name or by folder. Concluded is grouped by day, over the last 30 days.

The first time you open the pane, a short explanation sits at the top: what it is, how it works, what it is for. **Got it** puts it away, and the ⓘ beside the title brings it back.

## A session, at a glance

Each session shows its name, the folder it runs in, where it stands and for how long, and when it started, in words: *Started this morning at 09:05*, *yesterday at 18:00*, *on 3 October*. A Codex session without a name goes by the first line of its first message, and by *Codex session* until it has one. A name that starts with an emoji, as names often do, wears it as its mark; the others wear their first letter on a colour of their own.

Once Lancetta has seen a session work for a minute, a bar splits its time between **working** and **waiting on you**, as far as Lancetta saw it.

## What you can do with one

- **Open** opens the session in your terminal: a Claude Code background session as `claude attach` would, and a Codex session that is not working as `codex resume` would, in the folder it ran in, even after its terminal closed.
- **Done** takes the post-it down **without closing the session**: it stays in Claude Code or Codex, and its post-it comes back by itself if it starts working again. Done is not offered while a session is working.
- **Put back** puts a concluded post-it back on the wall, while its session is still there.
- A right-click offers **Rename…**, a name of your own for the post-it, and **Copy Attach Command**, or **Copy Resume Command** on a Codex session: the command Open runs, for a terminal of your choosing.

A concluded session can still be opened, and deleted:

- **Open** runs `codex resume` on a Codex session, `claude attach` on a Claude Code background session that is still there, and otherwise `claude --resume` on its conversation, in the folder it ran in. A right-click copies that command.
- **Delete…** deletes the session itself, after a confirmation that says what goes. On a Codex session it is Codex's own delete, for good. On a Claude Code session it is `claude rm`, then its conversation moves to the Trash, where the Finder can put it back; `claude --resume` no longer finds it while it is there. A session still running is left alone, and the pane says why. Lancetta forgets it too. Delete is in the window only: the panel has no room to say what it did.

A session you remove in Claude Code moves to Concluded by itself. Lancetta waits until it has been missing for half a minute of the Mac's awake time before believing it, and for longer when every session goes missing at once, which is more often a restart than an empty wall. A session that closes by itself, a terminal that closed or a Codex session its background service let go, moves there too, as *Closed*, at the time it left the list. Leaving the list says nothing about whether the work is finished, so a closed session that shows up again, resumed in another terminal, goes back up where it stood.

## What the wall cannot see of Codex

- **A terminal closing, straight away.** Codex keeps a session loaded in its background service for a while after its terminal closes, so for that while its post-it still reads as waiting on you, and it goes to Concluded as *Closed* only once Codex lets it go.
- **`/new` in a Codex terminal.** It starts another session, so the post-it of the one before stays up until you call it done: nothing in a Codex session ties it to the terminal it ran in.

## Saved

The agents keep their sessions after they are off the wall, and some of them pile up. The **Saved** card lists them, as each agent reports them when Lancetta asks it, and follows the switch at the top:

- **Claude Code's background sessions that have ended**, with what each still keeps on disk. **Remove…** runs `claude rm`, the way Claude Code documents: the folder goes, and a worktree the session made unless it holds work not pushed, which Claude Code refuses on its own. The conversation stays, for `claude --resume`. **Delete…** does the same, then moves the conversation to the Trash. **Select Finished** ticks the ones that finished, never one that stopped waiting for your answer.
- **Codex's saved sessions**: your conversations, and in a group of its own, closed until you open it, the ones every `codex exec` and `codex review` leaves behind, with the threads reviews run in. **Select Commands** ticks those, never a conversation. **Archive…** is Codex's own archive: the session leaves Codex's list, its file is compressed, and `codex unarchive` with its id brings it back. **Delete…** is Codex's own delete, for good. The sessions Codex has archived sit in a group of their own, where Delete still reaches them.

Every row opens in your terminal, as Open does on the wall. A Codex session is never archived or deleted while Codex has it open, while a program has its file open, when it was written to within the hour, or when an agent it spawned is any of these, since Codex takes a session's agents with it. Each one is looked at again just before its turn, and the pane says how many went and why any stayed. Codex's side reads through the `codex` [Settings → Sources](/docs/settings#sources) names, and shows nothing until it is found.

These used to be two cards in [Maintenance](/docs/maintenance), which no longer shows them.

## Working at once

Sessions of one agent working side by side spend its window together. When two or more of them are working and the window is heading for a stop before its reset, the [stop suggestion](/docs/suggestions#what-helps) says so first: *2 sessions of Claude are working at once: pause the ones that can wait.* It counts only from lists read now, never from a wall left from before.

## Without opening the window

- **In the panel**, a **Sessions** card joins the deck while any session is up, and opens into the first of them, each with Open and Done.
- **In the menu bar**, the sessions can have [an item of their own](/docs/settings#panel), off until you turn it on: the Lancetta icon and how many sessions, Claude Code's and Codex's, wait on you. A click opens the panel with the sessions alone, as the same four cards and the same switch: the first three open in place, and Saved opens the window on it. A right-click is the command menu, as on Lancetta's own item.

## When the wall is not current

A list Lancetta could not read is never counted as now. With no list read, every figure on the wall becomes a dash, and a line says why: the list could not be read, Claude Code took too long to answer, Codex did not answer with its list of sessions, the agent is not on this Mac, no agent is watched in [Settings](/docs/settings#agents), or the list has not been read since launch. With one agent's list read and the other's not, the figure counts the one read, and says it is partial: *partial: Codex list not read*. The sessions it last knew stay on the wall, and **Why, and what to do…** opens what went wrong. The three codes are on [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting).

An agent you stop watching in Settings › Agents takes its sessions off the wall, and a line says so; they are kept for when you watch it again.

## What it keeps

The wall and the record of concluded sessions are two files on this Mac, and nothing in them leaves it. A concluded session's record keeps the identifiers Open and Delete need, and Delete removes it. What each one holds, and how to clear the record, is on [Privacy](/docs/privacy#what-it-writes) and in [Settings → Privacy](/docs/settings#privacy).
