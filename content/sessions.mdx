# Sessions

Run a few sessions at once, with Claude Code and with Codex, in terminals and in the background, and the hard part stops being the work: it is remembering **which one is waiting for you**. The **Sessions** pane of [the window](/docs/the-window) keeps every session you started as a post-it on a wall and says where each one stands, so the next one to pick up is the first one you see.

They are the agents' sessions, not Lancetta's: nothing here starts one or closes one. At every refresh Lancetta reads the list each agent keeps of its sessions, and changes nothing in either:

- **Claude Code**: the list Claude Code keeps of its sessions, in the background and in a terminal.
- **Codex**: the sessions open in Codex's shared background service, which Codex's terminal app runs every session through from Codex 0.163. Nothing is installed into Codex for it: no plugin, no hook, no line in its configuration. A `codex exec` run, a subagent's thread and a session Claude Code started in Codex are not sessions you sit at, and stay off the wall.

How each list is read, and what is not, is on [How it reads each agent](/docs/how-it-reads#your-sessions-for-the-wall). On a wall that holds both agents' sessions, each post-it carries its agent's mark in the corner of its badge.

## Three places on the wall

- **Your turn**: the session finished its turn, asks you something, stopped or failed, or its terminal closed. The card says how long the one waiting longest has waited.
- **Working**: the agent is on it.
- **Concluded**: the sessions you called done, and the ones you removed in Claude Code. The card counts today's, and under it the last seven days.

Each card opens into its sessions, **as cards or as a list**: one switch for all three, kept from one launch to the next. A menu orders them: oldest first, newest first, by when they started, by name or by folder. Concluded is grouped by day, over the last 30 days.

The first time you open the pane, a short explanation sits at the top: what it is, how it works, what it is for. **Got it** puts it away, and the ⓘ beside the title brings it back.

## A session, at a glance

Each session shows its name, the folder it runs in, where it stands and for how long, and when it started, in words: *Started this morning at 09:05*, *yesterday at 18:00*, *on 3 October*. A Codex session without a name goes by the first line of its first message, and by *Codex session* until it has one. A name that starts with an emoji, as names often do, wears it as its mark; the others wear their first letter on a colour of their own.

Once Lancetta has seen a session work for a minute, a bar splits its time between **working** and **waiting on you**, as far as Lancetta saw it.

## What you can do with one

- **Open** opens the session in your terminal: a Claude Code background session as `claude attach` would, and a Codex session that is not working as `codex resume` would, in the folder it ran in, even after its terminal closed.
- **Done** takes the post-it down **without closing the session**: it stays in Claude Code or Codex, and its post-it comes back by itself if it starts working again. Done is not offered while a session is working.
- **Put back** puts a concluded post-it back on the wall, while its session is still there.
- A right-click offers **Rename…**, a name of your own for the post-it, and **Copy Attach Command**, or **Copy Resume Command** on a Codex session: the command Open runs, for a terminal of your choosing.

A session you remove in Claude Code moves to Concluded by itself. Lancetta waits until it has been missing for half a minute of the Mac's awake time before believing it, and for longer when every session goes missing at once, which is more often a restart than an empty wall. A session in a terminal that leaves the list, Claude Code's or Codex's, stays up as *Its terminal closed* until you call it done: leaving the list says nothing about whether the work is finished.

## What the wall cannot see of Codex

- **A terminal closing, straight away.** Codex keeps a session loaded in its background service for a while after its terminal closes, so for that while its post-it still reads as waiting on you, and *Its terminal closed* only once Codex lets it go.
- **`/new` in a Codex terminal.** It starts another session, so the post-it of the one before stays up until you call it done: nothing in a Codex session ties it to the terminal it ran in.

## Working at once

Sessions of one agent working side by side spend its window together. When two or more of them are working and the window is heading for a stop before its reset, the [stop suggestion](/docs/suggestions#what-helps) says so first: *2 sessions of Claude are working at once: pause the ones that can wait.* It counts only from lists read now, never from a wall left from before.

## Without opening the window

- **In the panel**, a **Sessions** card joins the deck while any session is up, and opens into the first of them, each with Open and Done.
- **In the menu bar**, the sessions can have [an item of their own](/docs/settings#panel), off until you turn it on: the Lancetta icon and how many sessions, Claude Code's and Codex's, wait on you. A click opens the panel with the sessions alone, as the same three cards, each opening in place. A right-click is the command menu, as on Lancetta's own item.

## When the wall is not current

A list Lancetta could not read is never counted as now. With no list read, every figure on the wall becomes a dash, and a line says why: the list could not be read, Claude Code took too long to answer, Codex did not answer with its list of sessions, the agent is not on this Mac, no agent is watched in [Settings](/docs/settings#agents), or the list has not been read since launch. With one agent's list read and the other's not, the figure counts the one read, and says it is partial: *partial: Codex list not read*. The sessions it last knew stay on the wall, and **Why, and what to do…** opens what went wrong. The three codes are on [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting).

An agent you stop watching in Settings › Agents takes its sessions off the wall, and a line says so; they are kept for when you watch it again.

## What it keeps

The wall and the record of concluded sessions are two files on this Mac, and nothing in them leaves it. What each one holds, and how to clear the record, is on [Privacy](/docs/privacy#what-it-writes) and in [Settings → Privacy](/docs/settings#privacy).
