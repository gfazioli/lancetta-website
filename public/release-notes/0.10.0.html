Lancetta 0.10.0

Release notes

The panel had seven controls in three places: a pin alone on a row of its own, two command rows, and four small buttons along the bottom. It now has five in two, and it is a row shorter for it.

The top of the panel says how fresh the numbers are

The strip across the top reads Updated 14s ago — how long since Lancetta last read an agent — and clicking it is Refresh now. Refresh used to be a row at the foot of the panel, as far as it could be from the only thing that makes you want it: a number that might be old. Now the age and the command are one control.

It spins while the read is on its way, and the panel stays open while it does, so you can watch the numbers move. ⌘R does the same, and no longer closes the panel either. Once three polls — and at least three minutes — go by without a reading, the timestamp turns amber: that is the moment a click is worth making.

That line is the panel's one clock. A card used to carry an age of its own as well, so a Claude card read from the status-line file said seen 10s ago under Updated 7s ago — two facts from two instants that read as one fact printed twice. A card now shows its age only when it trails the top line by more than a minute and a half: after an evening with no Claude Code session open, seen 3h17m ago under Updated 5s ago, and the gap is the information.

Settings beside the pin, and a shorter foot

The gear moved up next to the pin, where window chrome lives. The foot of the panel is down to Open Lancetta… and Quit.

Right-click for every command

Right-click Lancetta in the menu bar and you get the classic menu: Refresh Now, Open Lancetta…, Settings…, Check for Updates…, About Lancetta and Quit Lancetta. A left click still opens the panel. It works with the panel pinned, too.

Check for Updates and About moved here from the panel because each one repeated a Settings pane, and the first nearly always answers "you are up to date".

An update shows up when there is one

When a new version is out, a button naming it appears at the top of the panel, and it opens the update prompt. The rest of the time it is not there at all — a button that is always on screen and almost never has anything to say teaches you to stop looking at it.
