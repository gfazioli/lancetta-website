Lancetta 0.3.4

Release notes

The password dialog is gone

Claude Code rewrites its keychain item every time it renews its own sign-in, and the rewrite clears the permission you gave Lancetta. Every background check after that was an authorisation failure — which macOS answers with a dialog asking for your login password, roughly once an hour, while you are doing something else. Measured on one Mac: six of them in three days, and four "Always Allow" grants wiped within the hour of being given.

Lancetta now reads your sign-in without ever letting macOS put a dialog on screen when it polls. The read fails quietly instead, and the Claude card says the sign-in was renewed, with a Reconnect button beside it. macOS asks only when you ask it to.

Verified by running the build for three polling cycles and counting: zero dialogs, three calm rows.

Two things that uncovered

  • A renewed sign-in used to read as a failure. It came out as "the account couldn't be reached", in red, over an account that was perfectly reachable. It now has its own code — CLAUDE-12 — and is drawn as a state you can fix in one click rather than as a fault.
  • The Usage pane's empty-chart line could name a Codex failure while the Claude filter was on.

Underneath

Every line Lancetta records about a quota now says which copy of the app wrote it, and through which route. A Mac that briefly ran two copies used to produce a history that read like a quota draining away when it was simply two honest readings interleaved.

Percentages are now rounded where they are recorded rather than where they are drawn: nine lines in one history file had been written purely because of floating-point noise — the app had recorded a change that never happened.

And the measurement the roadmap was waiting for

The advice on the roadmap was gated on one question, published in advance: across a reset boundary, does a quota window fall in one step, or drain back gradually? A slope meant deleting the feature rather than rewording it, because advice built on a slope tells you to spend quota that is not actually expiring.

It came back a step. 1,890 readings, both agents, both windows; across twenty-five windows the floor of the series never fell, and the sharpest boundary went from 100% to zero in forty-two seconds. So the advice can be built, and the work has started.
