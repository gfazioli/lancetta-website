Lancetta 0.6.2

Release notes

Two changes, one asked for and one found in a log.

Point at it, if you would rather not click

Settings › General › Menu › Open the menu on hover. Off unless you turn it on: a panel that opens while the pointer is merely crossing the menu bar on its way somewhere else is an interruption for everyone who did not ask for one.

It waits a quarter of a second, which is not a round number for its own sake — a pointer travelling to another app's menu-bar item crosses ours in a few dozen milliseconds, and a panel that opened on the way past would be in the way every time.

Opened by the pointer, the panel does not take the keyboard: moving a mouse should not pull the focus out of what you are typing, so Escape and ⌘R are not live yet. Click the item and it is yours — the panel takes the keyboard, its shortcuts work, and it stays open until you click away, exactly as a clicked-open panel always did. Clicking a row inside it works either way: Refresh now does refresh.

A pause no longer freezes the card for minutes

When Claude's account answers "too many reads" in the first moments after Lancetta starts, there is no number on the card yet — so the pause is the whole card, and it used to sit there for a minute and a half at a time, with Refresh now apparently doing nothing. Reported twice, both times on the first launch after an update, and escaped by quitting the app.

The account sends that refusal with no wait attached, and Lancetta took the absence of a number as a wait of zero seconds: the pause never grew, and the sentence under it rounded up and promised a minute that nothing was counting.

Now: with nothing on the card, Lancetta asks again after ten seconds, then twenty, forty, up to two minutes — and comes back for it by itself rather than waiting for the next scheduled reading. Once there is a number on screen nothing changes: a pause is invisible, because the reading you can see is kept and shown with its age. Refresh now gets through a pause as well, and the sentence says "in a few seconds" when it means seconds.

Also

  • The panel's Settings page gained a Menu section; everything else there is where it was.
  • A development hatch that had been quietly ignoring half of what it was given now reads it properly. Nothing user-facing, but it is why a flag in Settings is read the same way everywhere.
